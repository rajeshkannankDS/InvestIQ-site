STANDALONE VERDICT (analyst view, Sep 2026)
AVOID · 12M horizon

A genuinely large and useful recycling business (India's biggest PET-bottle recycler, 16–18% of national PET waste, ₹1,482 Cr of sales) is being offered at P/E 46.3 while delivering ROE 3.30% and ROCE 5.58% — returns below the cost of capital. Profits are shrinking (5Y −1%, 3Y −16%, TTM −38%), OPM fell from 14% to 10% in one year, one quarter in the last twelve actually lost money (Sep-2025), borrowings of ₹496 Cr cost ₹40 Cr of interest a year (28% of operating profit), and — most seriously — 31.9% of the promoter's shares are pledged after the promoter had already cut the stake from 42.32% to 36.44% in 2024. Composite score 3.1/10 falls below the 4.0 avoid threshold on every valuation and quality test.

Re-entry conditions (none currently met): promoter pledge released below 10% (ideally 0%) · ROE back above 12% and ROCE above 15% for a full year · OPM sustained ≥14% with positive profit CAGR · borrowings below ₹400 Cr with interest under ₹30 Cr · until all four hold, this stays off the buy list regardless of price.

1. Company / Stock Metadata

Section 1 — The View (plain English)
  • A real, defensible business: GANECOS is the largest PET-bottle recycling company in India — it recycles 16–18% of the country's entire PET bottle waste, converts 150,000+ MTPA of PET waste into Recycled Polyester Staple Fibre (RPSF), spun yarn and dyed texturised yarn, and recycled 8+ billion bottles in FY25.IMPORTANT
  • Identity is old and clean (incorporated 1987 as Ganesh Polytex, renamed Oct-2011, dual-listed, part of BSE Consumer Discretionary and BSE 1000 indices) — but the ownership picture is not clean: promoter pledge 31.9% and a promoter stake that was slashed 42→36 in 2024.IMPORTANT
  • Screener's own verdict lines: pro — healthy dividend payout 18.1%; cons — low return on equity 5.80% over last 3 years, promoters have pledged 31.9% of their holding. Two cons, one pro.
  • Smart money is divided: FIIs climbed from 1.67% to 10.38% while DIIs fell from 22.89% to 15.07% over 12 quarters — institutions are rotating, not accumulating together.

⚠ Pledge red flag — say it plainly Fact

Promoters have pledged 31.9% of their holding. That means roughly a third of the promoter shares are borrowed against — the promoter has taken loans using the stock as collateral. This is a standard small-cap red flag for three reasons: (1) if the share price falls toward the 52-week low of ₹653 (from ₹997 today), lenders can issue margin calls and sell pledged shares into the market, accelerating the fall; (2) it tells you the promoter needed cash outside the company; (3) it sits on top of a stake that was already cut from 42.32% to 36.44% in 2024. Combined with DII exits (22.89% → 15.07%), the domestic institutional read on this stock is negative.

Action: check the pledge % in every quarterly shareholding pattern. Any print above 31.9% or any promoter stake below 39.33% makes this an automatic AVOID regardless of valuation.
